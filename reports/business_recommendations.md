# Business Recommendations

## 1. Create a Workload Risk Review

Employees with high project loads and high monthly hours should be flagged for workload review. HR and operations should review whether these employees have enough support, whether project assignments are balanced, and whether deadlines are creating avoidable burnout risk.

**Recommended action:** Build a monthly workload report by department that tracks project count, monthly hours, and recent staffing changes.

## 2. Investigate Mid-Tenure Attrition

Employees around three to five years of tenure may be at a career decision point. If they do not see advancement, role growth, or compensation movement, they may be more likely to leave.

**Recommended action:** Create a retention review for employees approaching four years of tenure, focused on promotion readiness, role fit, internal mobility, and career goals.

## 3. Review Promotion Equity and Timing

Promotion history should be treated as a business policy signal. If strong employees remain unpromoted while taking on more projects, the company may unintentionally increase attrition risk.

**Recommended action:** Compare promotion rates by department, tenure, evaluation score, and salary band to identify bottlenecks.

## 4. Separate High Performance From Overwork

The analysis suggests that evaluation outcomes may be connected to heavy workloads. This creates a risk that employees are rewarded for unsustainable working patterns.

**Recommended action:** Ask managers to review whether top evaluation scores are consistently associated with high monthly hours and whether staffing plans need adjustment.

## 5. Use Satisfaction Data as an Early Support Signal

Low satisfaction is strongly associated with attrition, but it should be used carefully. Satisfaction surveys are most useful when they lead to support and process improvement rather than employee labeling.

**Recommended action:** Use satisfaction changes to trigger manager coaching, workload conversations, and employee support resources.

## 6. Treat the Model as Decision Support

The attrition model should not make employment decisions. It should help HR prioritize outreach and identify operational patterns that may be driving turnover.

**Recommended action:** Create a governance rule that model predictions can only be used for supportive retention planning, not punitive decisions.
